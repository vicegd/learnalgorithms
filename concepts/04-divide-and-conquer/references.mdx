# References: Divide and Conquer

Books, articles and resources to go deeper into the lessons of this chapter. The textbooks are listed in order of relevance to the course.

## Course readings

These readings (in Spanish) are part of the course materials:

- *Book - divide y vencerás* and *Esquema básico - divide and conquer*.

## Textbooks

- G. Brassard and P. Bratley. *Fundamentals of Algorithmics*. Prentice Hall, 1996 (Spanish edition: *Fundamentos de algoritmia*, 1997). The chapter on divide and conquer uses the same scheme as this course.
- R. Guerequeta and A. Vallecillo. *Técnicas de diseño de algoritmos*. Universidad de Málaga, 1998. Free online: [lcc.uma.es/~av/Libro](http://www.lcc.uma.es/~av/Libro/). The chapter "Divide y vencerás" presents the scheme and many solved problems, in Spanish.
- T. H. Cormen, C. E. Leiserson, R. L. Rivest and C. Stein. *Introduction to Algorithms*, 4th ed. MIT Press, 2022. Divide and conquer, recurrences and the master theorem, Strassen's algorithm, and selection in linear time.
- S. Dasgupta, C. Papadimitriou and U. Vazirani. *Algorithms*. McGraw-Hill, 2006. A short and very readable chapter on divide and conquer, including fast multiplication and the master theorem.
- J. Kleinberg and É. Tardos. *Algorithm Design*. Addison-Wesley, 2005. A chapter on divide and conquer with counting inversions and the closest pair of points.

## Original sources

- A. Karatsuba and Yu. Ofman. "Multiplication of many-digital numbers by automatic computers". *Doklady Akademii Nauk SSSR*, 145(2):293–294, 1962.
- V. Strassen. "Gaussian elimination is not optimal". *Numerische Mathematik*, 13:354–356, 1969.
- C. A. R. Hoare. "Algorithm 65: Find". *Communications of the ACM*, 4(7):321–322, 1961. The origin of Quickselect.
- M. Blum, R. W. Floyd, V. Pratt, R. L. Rivest and R. E. Tarjan. "Time bounds for selection". *Journal of Computer and System Sciences*, 7(4):448–461, 1973. Selection in linear worst-case time (median of medians).

## Online resources

- [Recursion trees and recurrences (VisuAlgo)](https://visualgo.net/en/recursion): draws the recursion tree of any recursive function.
