# Course Overview

These lessons cover the theory of **algorithmics**: how to measure the cost of an algorithm, how to analyze it before running it, and the main strategies for designing efficient algorithms. They follow the Algorithmics course of the Software Engineering degree, but every lesson is self-contained: you can read them in order as a course, or jump to any topic as a reference.

The code of the examples, with tests and time measurements, is in the course repository [vicegd/algorithms](https://github.com/vicegd/algorithms) (Java).

## How the lessons work

- Every claim about running times has been **measured**, and every example has been **executed**. Where a lesson gives a number of operations or a time, it is a real measurement, not an estimate.
- Most lessons end with **self-check questions** (open them to see the answer), a list of **key ideas**, and **practice** or **try it yourself** exercises. Exercises from the seminars and lab sessions appear without solutions.
- Chapters end with an **exercises** page whose solutions are grouped at the end.
- Mathematical notation is kept to what is needed: sums, logarithms and the asymptotic notation introduced in chapter 1.

## Suggested order

Chapters 1 and 2 are the base for everything else: read them first and in order. In chapter 3, divide and conquer, greedy algorithms and dynamic programming can be read in that order (each one compares itself with the previous ones). Chapter 4 builds on brute force and dynamic programming. Chapter 5 (parallelism) only needs divide and conquer, so it can be read right after the first lessons of chapter 3. Chapter 6 is a reference: consult it whenever a lesson relies on a sum, a logarithm or a recurrence, or read it alongside chapter 1.
