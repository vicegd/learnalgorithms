References: Sorting

Books, articles and resources to go deeper into the lessons of this chapter. The textbooks are listed in order of relevance to the course.

Course readings​

  • Book - ordenación (in Spanish, part of the course materials): the sorting algorithms of the chapter and their analysis.

Textbooks​

  • R. Sedgewick and K. Wayne. Algorithms, 4th ed. Addison-Wesley, 2011. Its chapter on sorting, with Java implementations, is one of the best introductions to the subject.
  • T. H. Cormen, C. E. Leiserson, R. L. Rivest and C. Stein. Introduction to Algorithms, 4th ed. MIT Press, 2022. Insertion sort, Mergesort, Heapsort, Quicksort (with the analysis of its average case), the lower bound for comparison sorting, and counting and radix sort.
  • D. E. Knuth. The Art of Computer Programming, Volume 3: Sorting and Searching, 2nd ed. Addison-Wesley, 1998. The encyclopedic reference on sorting, including the minimum number of comparisons needed to sort small arrays.
  • A. Levitin. Introduction to the Design and Analysis of Algorithms, 3rd ed. Pearson, 2012. Selection and bubble sort as brute force, insertion sort as decrease-and-conquer, and Mergesort and Quicksort as divide and conquer.

Original sources​

  • D. L. Shell. "A high-speed sorting procedure". Communications of the ACM, 2(7):30–32, 1959.
  • C. A. R. Hoare. "Quicksort". The Computer Journal, 5(1):10–16, 1962.
  • J. W. J. Williams. "Algorithm 232: Heapsort". Communications of the ACM, 7(6):347–348, 1964.
  • J. L. Bentley and M. D. McIlroy. "Engineering a sort function". Software: Practice and Experience, 23(11):1249–1265, 1993. The design of a practical Quicksort (median of three, three-way partitioning), which influenced the sorting methods of most standard libraries.

Online resources​

  • Sorting algorithms visualized (VisuAlgo): step-by-step animations of every algorithm in the chapter.
  • Comparison sorting algorithms (David Galles): interactive animations.
