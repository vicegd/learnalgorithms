8. Branch and Bound

Exploring the most promising states first and discarding those whose bound shows they cannot beat the best solution: live nodes, priority queues, bounds and heuristics, A*, and classic problems such as task assignment, knapsack, sliding puzzles and the traveling salesman.

The Branch and Bound Scheme

The name “branch and bound” was introduced by Little, Murty, Sweeney and Karel in 1963.

Proofs

Branch and bound finds the optimal solution only if its bounds and heuristics are valid. This page proves the facts used in the lessons: why the bounds of the examples are valid, why best-first branch and bound and A\* stop at the optimum, and why the puzzle heuristics and the parity condition work. Safe pruning with a bound is proved in the backtracking chapter.
