# References: Algorithm Design Paradigms

Books, articles and resources to go deeper into the lessons of this chapter. The textbooks are listed in order of relevance to the course.

## Course readings

These readings (in Spanish) are part of the course materials:

- *Book - divide y vencerás* and *Esquema básico - divide and conquer*.
- *Book - devoradores* (greedy algorithms).
- *Book - programación dinámica*.

## Textbooks

- G. Brassard and P. Bratley. *Fundamentals of Algorithmics*. Prentice Hall, 1996 (Spanish edition: *Fundamentos de algoritmia*, 1997). Separate chapters on greedy algorithms, divide and conquer and dynamic programming, with the same schemes used in this course.
- R. Guerequeta and A. Vallecillo. *Técnicas de diseño de algoritmos*. Universidad de Málaga, 1998. Free online: [lcc.uma.es/~av/Libro](http://www.lcc.uma.es/~av/Libro/). Each technique is presented with a general scheme and many solved problems, in Spanish.
- J. Kleinberg and É. Tardos. *Algorithm Design*. Addison-Wesley, 2005. The best text for learning to **prove** that greedy algorithms are optimal (exchange arguments, "greedy stays ahead") and to design dynamic programming algorithms.
- T. H. Cormen, C. E. Leiserson, R. L. Rivest and C. Stein. *Introduction to Algorithms*, 4th ed. MIT Press, 2022. Divide and conquer and the master theorem, dynamic programming (rod cutting, matrix chains, LCS) and greedy algorithms (activity selection, Huffman codes).
- A. Levitin. *Introduction to the Design and Analysis of Algorithms*, 3rd ed. Pearson, 2012. Chapter 3 covers brute force and exhaustive search; later chapters cover each design technique.
- S. Dasgupta, C. Papadimitriou and U. Vazirani. *Algorithms*. McGraw-Hill, 2006. A short and very readable text, with excellent chapters on divide and conquer and dynamic programming.

## Original sources

- A. Karatsuba and Yu. Ofman. "Multiplication of many-digital numbers by automatic computers". *Doklady Akademii Nauk SSSR*, 145(2):293–294, 1962.
- V. Strassen. "Gaussian elimination is not optimal". *Numerische Mathematik*, 13:354–356, 1969.
- C. A. R. Hoare. "Algorithm 65: Find". *Communications of the ACM*, 4(7):321–322, 1961. The origin of Quickselect.
- M. Blum, R. W. Floyd, V. Pratt, R. L. Rivest and R. E. Tarjan. "Time bounds for selection". *Journal of Computer and System Sciences*, 7(4):448–461, 1973. Selection in linear worst-case time (median of medians).
- D. A. Huffman. "A method for the construction of minimum-redundancy codes". *Proceedings of the IRE*, 40(9):1098–1101, 1952.
- J. B. Kruskal. "On the shortest spanning subtree of a graph and the traveling salesman problem". *Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society*, 7(1):48–50, 1956.
- E. W. Dijkstra. "A note on two problems in connexion with graphs". *Numerische Mathematik*, 1:269–271, 1959.
- R. Bellman. *Dynamic Programming*. Princeton University Press, 1957. The book that named the technique and stated the principle of optimality.
- R. W. Floyd. "Algorithm 97: Shortest path". *Communications of the ACM*, 5(6):345, 1962.

## Online resources

- [Recursion trees and recurrences (VisuAlgo)](https://visualgo.net/en/recursion): draws the recursion tree of any recursive function, including the Fibonacci numbers and binomial coefficients.
- [Dynamic programming visualizations (David Galles)](https://www.cs.usfca.edu/~galles/visualization/Algorithms.html): animated tables for Fibonacci, LCS and other problems.
