References: Foundations of Algorithms

Books, articles and resources to go deeper into the lessons of this chapter. The textbooks are listed in order of relevance to the course.

Course readings​

These readings (in Spanish) are part of the course materials:

  • Book - complejidades: efficiency of algorithms, asymptotic notation and the analysis of loops.
  • Esquema básico - cálculo de n & t: a summary of how to calculate the size of the problem and the execution time.

Textbooks​

  • G. Brassard and P. Bratley. Fundamentals of Algorithmics. Prentice Hall, 1996. Spanish edition: Fundamentos de algoritmia, Prentice Hall, 1997. The chapters on the efficiency of algorithms and asymptotic notation follow the same approach as this course.
  • A. Levitin. Introduction to the Design and Analysis of Algorithms, 3rd ed. Pearson, 2012. Chapter 2 covers the analysis framework, asymptotic notation, the analysis of non-recursive and recursive algorithms, and empirical analysis.
  • T. H. Cormen, C. E. Leiserson, R. L. Rivest and C. Stein. Introduction to Algorithms, 4th ed. MIT Press, 2022. The reference text for asymptotic notation and the analysis of algorithms, with full proofs.
  • R. Guerequeta and A. Vallecillo. Técnicas de diseño de algoritmos. Servicio de Publicaciones de la Universidad de Málaga, 1998. Free online edition: lcc.uma.es/~av/Libro. Its first chapter covers the complexity of algorithms, in Spanish.
  • M. A. Weiss. Data Structures and Algorithm Analysis in Java, 3rd ed. Pearson, 2012. Algorithm analysis and the data structures of the Java collections.
  • J. Bloch. Effective Java, 3rd ed. Addison-Wesley, 2018. How to use the Java collections correctly.

Original sources and classic texts​

  • D. E. Knuth. The Art of Computer Programming, Volume 1: Fundamental Algorithms, 3rd ed. Addison-Wesley, 1997. The definition of algorithm (finiteness, definiteness, input, output, effectiveness) used in the first lesson comes from its first section.
  • D. E. Knuth. "Big Omicron and big Omega and big Theta". ACM SIGACT News, 8(2):18–24, 1976. The article that fixed the modern meaning of OOO, Ω\OmegaΩ and Θ\ThetaΘ.

Online resources​

  • VisuAlgo: animations of data structures and algorithms.
  • Algorithms, 4th edition (Sedgewick and Wayne): the companion site of the book, with Java code for every algorithm.
