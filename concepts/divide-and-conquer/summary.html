Summary: Divide and Conquer

Everything on one page, to review the chapter before moving on.

Key ideas​

  • Divide and conquer divides a problem into smaller subproblems of the same kind, solves them recursively and combines their solutions (The Divide and Conquer Scheme).
  • The number of subproblems should be small, and the subproblems independent. When they repeat, dynamic programming is the right tool.
  • With a single subproblem (a=1a = 1a=1) the process is a reduction, often better written as a loop. Recursion uses stack memory proportional to its depth.
  • Count the recursive calls that are executed, not the ones written (Analyzing Divide and Conquer).
  • Discarding the subproblems that cannot contain the answer is what makes binary search and Quickselect fast (Majority, Mode and Median).

When to use it​

  • The problem splits into independent subproblems of the same kind, and their solutions combine cheaply.
  • Avoid it when the subproblems overlap (naive Fibonacci): use dynamic programming instead.
  • Prefer division schemes (n/bn/bn/b) to subtraction schemes (n−bn - bn−b) with several subproblems, which are exponential.
