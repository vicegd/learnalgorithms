2. Sorting

Sorting, one of the most studied problems in computing: the criteria to compare sorting algorithms, the simple algorithms (bubble, insertion, selection), Quicksort, the efficient alternatives (Shellsort, Heapsort, Mergesort, Radix sort), the theoretical limit of comparison-based sorting, and how to choose an algorithm in practice.

The Sorting Problem

Formal statement of the sorting problem and the criteria to evaluate sorting algorithms: complexity, comparisons, swaps, stability, and memory.

Bubble Sort

Bubble sort, inversions, sentinel optimization, and bidirectional (cocktail) bubble sort with traces and measurements.

Insertion Sort

Direct insertion sort, why it is adaptive on nearly sorted data, binary insertion sort, and empirical measurements.

Selection Sort

Direct selection sort, why it performs at most n - 1 swaps, why it is not stable, and how it leads to Heapsort.

Quicksort

Partitioning, pivot selection with median-of-three, average vs. worst-case analysis, and practical engineering improvements.

Beyond Quadratic Sorting

Shellsort, Heapsort, Mergesort, the Omega(n log n) lower bound for comparison sorting, and linear-time non-comparison sorts.

Comparing Sorting Algorithms

Head-to-head complexity and empirical benchmarks of sorting algorithms, external sorting, and how to choose one in practice.

Summary

Chapter 2 summary: sorting criteria, complexity table at a glance, and practical selection guidelines.

Exercises

Exercises with solutions on simple sorts, Quicksort, efficient sorting algorithms, lower bounds, and algorithm selection.

Proofs

Formal proofs for Chapter 2: correctness of sorting algorithms, the n log n comparison lower bound, and linear-time heap construction.

References

Course readings, textbooks, and original papers on sorting algorithms.
