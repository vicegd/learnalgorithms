# References: Dynamic Programming

Books, articles and resources to go deeper into the lessons of this chapter. The textbooks are listed in order of relevance to the course.

## Textbooks

- G. Brassard and P. Bratley. *Fundamentals of Algorithmics*. Prentice Hall, 1996 (Spanish edition: *Fundamentos de algoritmia*, 1997). The chapter on dynamic programming uses the same scheme as this course.
- R. Guerequeta and A. Vallecillo. *Técnicas de diseño de algoritmos*. Universidad de Málaga, 1998. Free online: [lcc.uma.es/~av/Libro](http://www.lcc.uma.es/~av/Libro/). The chapter "Programación dinámica" presents the technique and many solved problems, in Spanish.
- J. Kleinberg and É. Tardos. *Algorithm Design*. Addison-Wesley, 2005. Excellent for learning to design dynamic programming algorithms: weighted scheduling, segmented least squares, knapsack and sequence alignment.
- T. H. Cormen, C. E. Leiserson, R. L. Rivest and C. Stein. *Introduction to Algorithms*, 4th ed. MIT Press, 2022. Rod cutting, matrix chain multiplication, the longest common subsequence and optimal binary search trees.
- S. Dasgupta, C. Papadimitriou and U. Vazirani. *Algorithms*. McGraw-Hill, 2006. A short and very readable chapter on dynamic programming.

## Original sources

- R. Bellman. *Dynamic Programming*. Princeton University Press, 1957. The book that named the technique and stated the principle of optimality.
- R. W. Floyd. "Algorithm 97: Shortest path". *Communications of the ACM*, 5(6):345, 1962.

## Online resources

- [Dynamic programming visualizations (David Galles)](https://www.cs.usfca.edu/~galles/visualization/Algorithms.html): animated tables for Fibonacci, LCS and other problems.
- [Recursion trees and recurrences (VisuAlgo)](https://visualgo.net/en/recursion): shows how the naive recursion of the Fibonacci numbers and binomial coefficients repeats subproblems.
